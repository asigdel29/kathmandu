# x13_y19 — चमती (Chamati), north part

- The tile is the northern half of Chamati, a dense residential district on the west bank of the Bishnumati river in the west of Kathmandu, a short distance east of Swayambhu hill. The mapped streets are named after the area: Chamati Marg, Chamati Bishnumati Kinar and Khusibu Bishnumati Kinar.
- Two roads follow the river bank: Khusibu Bishnumati Kinar (खुशिबु बिष्णुमती किनार), a secondary road that runs diagonally across the tile, and a tertiary road beside it (चमती बिष्णुमती किनार). Siddhicharan Sadak (सिद्धिचरण सडक) runs down the west side toward the Kathmandu Ring Road, which clips the north-west corner.
- About 2,400 footprints fill the tile, almost all of them RCC houses of three to five storeys packed along narrow lanes; a few small patches of farmland, a park (Khusibu Children Park) and a pitch break up the housing.
- The busiest shopfront street is Yekikaran Sadak on the east side, where a momo restaurant, a mart, a cyber shop and Prime Global School face the road.
- Three small places of worship are mapped as points rather than buildings: two Hindu Mahadev temples and a Buddhist stupa.
- Sensory detail: at street level the lanes are narrow concrete canyons of pastel and white walls, three to five storeys high, with only a strip of sky overhead.

Data notes: no footprint crosses a road in this tile, so nothing is removed. Default storeys are raised from 3 to 4. The three worship points do not sit inside a footprint, so each gets a shrine prop; prayer flags are placed around the stupa.
