# x14_y16 — भीमसेनथान (Bhimsenthan)

- The tile centres on Bhimsenthan, the quarter south-west of Hanuman Dhoka named after its Bhimsen temple. Bhimsen is the Newar god of traders and cooks; the three-storey rectangular temple stands at the north-west corner of the tile, with shop stalls on its ground floor.
- The north-west is the dense Newar core: lanes only a few metres wide (Phalchasa Pode Galli, Jor Ganesh Galli, Chickanmuga Marg, Lakhenanii Galli) packed with four-to-six-storey brick and plaster houses and a small Ganesh or Hindu shrine at nearly every junction.
- Dharma Marg in the north carries the Nepal Bank Limited central office buildings. Nepal Bank was founded in 1937 and is the first bank of Nepal; its head office is on Dharmapath.
- North-east of the bank, Pukhudha Marg, Khichapukhu Sadak and Bagdurbar Marg hold taller commercial blocks (Pashupati Plaza, People's Plaza, Standard Chartered Bank) and, at the eastern edge, the Life Care Hospital tower.
- The south is cut by the Tripureshwor Marg trunk road and Pachali Marg, which carry the larger school, government and commercial buildings (National Trading, Ambe Complex) in the south-west.
- Sensory detail: scooter horns echoing in lanes only a few metres wide, with small shrines at the corners.

Data notes: the OSM height tag on Jaisi Deval Temple (0.8 m) is a plinth height, so it is overridden with storey counts. No footprint in the source data crosses a road, so nothing is removed. Worship points that have no footprint of their own are placed as roadside shrines, and the three Buddhist points get prayer flags.
