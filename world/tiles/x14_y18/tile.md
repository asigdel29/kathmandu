# x14_y18 — तेङ्गल (Tengal)

- The tile covers the Thamel tourist quarter and its neighbours: Thamel Marg and Narsingh Chowk run north–south on the east side, Paknajol is the long road on the western side, Chhetrapati and J.P. Road close the south, and Kushlechaur Marg crosses the west.
- Thamel is a commercial neighbourhood of narrow alleys: trekking shops, travel agencies, guest houses, cafes and pubs, with scooters, taxis and rickshaws sharing lanes with walkers. The name comes from Tha Bahi, a Newar courtyard.
- Buildings here are mostly RCC hotels and shops of four to six storeys, not Newar brick, so the tile is set to the commercial zone; only the temple and bahal footprints keep brick style.
- Between the hotels sit older Newar courtyards (bahal) and wayside shrines, among them Maitripur Mahavihar (Kwa Bahal) off Thamel Marg and the stupa at Tahiti (Thahity) at the south edge.
- Mandala Street is the pedestrian lane of cafes and restaurants off Paryatan Marg.
- Sensory detail: scooter horns, shopkeepers calling and music from the bars in lanes lined with shop signs.

Data notes: three footprints that sit across the pedestrian lane at (700, 300) are removed; schools, the banquet hall and the museum are set to institutional or glass styles; OSM footprints in the tile are plain boxes, so the temples are only approximated by storeys and roof type.
