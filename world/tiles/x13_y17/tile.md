# x13_y17 — मरु (Maru)

- The tile sits on the Bishnumati river, which runs north to south through its eastern third. Most of the tile is the west bank between Dallu in the north and Kalimati in the south; the OSM neighbourhood point for Maru lies just beyond the east edge, on the far bank.
- The Bishnumati flows along the western side of old Kathmandu and is holy to both Hindus and Buddhists; the river road (Bishnumati Track Road, Pushpalal Path) is the tile's main traffic spine, with Dallu bridge carrying it over the water at the north edge.
- The west bank is dense, newer concrete housing at about four storeys, packed so tightly that lanes are a few metres wide. It is zoned suburb rather than old-core for that reason; only the narrow east strip keeps the brick look of the old Newar quarter.
- Kankeshwori, on the river bank, is the tile's shrine: a temple of the goddess Durga used by local Newa families for morning worship, reached by the Kankeshwori footbridge.
- Schools and offices break up the housing: Shining Path Public School, Kankeshwori Secondary School and the District Education Office on Chagal Marg.
- Sensory detail: after the shoulder-width lanes of the housing blocks, the river corridor opens into the widest sky in the tile, with the shrine and footbridge right at the water.

Data notes: the whole tile used to be rendered as old-core (red brick, pitched roofs), which does not match the concrete west bank. Sheds under 22 m² are set to one storey and small houses under 40 m² to two so they do not appear as towers. One 12 m² footprint drawn on Jiwan Smriti Marg is removed.
