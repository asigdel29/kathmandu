# x12_y15 — कुलेश्वर (Kuleshwor)

- The tile covers the Kuleshwor quarter in south-west Kathmandu, between the Ring Road and the Balkhu River. Kuleshwar Township began as a planned settlement, laid out by the Kathmandu Town Development Committee in the 1970s with plots for government employees; the grid of lanes (Gaurishankar Sadak, Lik Marg, Sunar Gau Marg, Nag Marg and others) still shows that planning.
- Almost every plot holds a multi-storey RCC house, packed in rows along 4–5 m lanes: about 2,600 footprints are mapped. Local writing notes how many temples the area has per household, and the tile holds several of them.
- A four-lane trunk road (mapped as रबिभवन and Lampati, 12 m wide) runs along the north edge; shops, a clinic and the ten-storey Mathuri Sadan stand beside it. This is the busiest street for signs and awnings.
- The ground rises to a terrace about 20 m above the tile's lowest point in the middle and falls away to the south, where the Balkhu River touches the edge and the Advanced College of Engineering and Management sits beside the Bhuwaneshwori Temple Park.
- Schools and kindergartens are scattered through the lanes, so the area is full of school-hour foot traffic.
- Sensory detail: temple bells and school bells over scooter horns from the Ring Road.

Data notes: 32 footprints under 8 m² are removed as slivers. Default storeys are raised to four to match the RCC infill. Temples, churches and kindergartens carry tags the style picker does not map, so they are styled by id. The one-storey block a684208226 is raised to two storeys because it sits across a 3 m slope.
