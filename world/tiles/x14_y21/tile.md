# x14_y21 — माछापोखरी (Machhapokhari)

- The tile covers Machhapokhari ("fish pond"), a junction quarter in north-west Kathmandu on the Kathmandu Ring Road (mapped as Kathmandu Ringroad, a trunk road) where the road from Balaju meets the Basantanagar and Mitranagar lanes. Gongabu's Naya Bus Park, the valley's long-distance bus terminal, lies just to the north-east of this junction, so the tile is full of buses, ticket counters and waiting passengers.
- Several roadside bus stops are mapped here: Machha Pokhari Bus Station, Dhading Bus Park, New Bus Stand and a stop for buses to Besisahar. Long-distance routes to Dhading, Nuwakot and Rasuwa used to load along these roadsides before the city told operators to run from inside the bus park.
- Around the junction, malls and stores (Kantipur Mall, Lotse Mall, Buddha Mall, Garden Mart) sit beside the Ring Road; behind them is a dense grid of about 2,500 mapped footprints, mostly four-storey RCC houses, apartment blocks and many small schools.
- A chain of small reservoir ponds and a stream run along the west edge of the tile; a river crosses the middle.
- Two worship sites are mapped: Harihar Kshetra, a Hindu site at the north edge, and a Buddhist gumba beside the Bishnumati bank lane.
- Sensory detail: diesel and horns from the Ring Road, conductors calling out destinations beside the bus stops.

Data notes: two footprints under 8 m² are removed as slivers, and six unnamed footprints with a 11–17 m lane drawn through them are removed. Default storeys are raised to four to match the RCC infill. The large unnamed building beside the New Bus Stand (a595777712) is styled as an institutional two-storey block; the three blue roofs next to it are bus canopies and are kept to one storey.
