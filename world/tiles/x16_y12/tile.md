# x16_y12 — थईना (Thaina)

- The tile covers the southern half of Patan (Lalitpur) around Patan Durbar Square: the royal palace and the line of temples facing it sit in the north-east, and the lanes of Mangal Bazar (मङ्गलबजार) run out of the square to the south-west.
- Patan Durbar Square is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. The main temples stand in a row facing the west front of the palace, with the stone Krishna Mandir, the Taleju bell, the octagonal stone Chyasim Deval and the wooden Hari Shankar temple among them; the square is paved in red brick.
- Around the square the streets are narrow Newari brick lanes of three to five storeys, with bahals (courtyard monasteries) and small shrines at almost every corner, and stone water spouts (hiti) such as Manga Hiti and Tusha Hiti still in use.
- The south and south-west edge of the tile, towards Lagankhel and Thaina-Prayagpokhari Road, turns to concrete blocks: Patan Hospital, Lalitpur Mall, banks, schools and shops along the wider roads.
- Sensory detail: shopfronts selling metalwork and carved souvenirs, flower sellers setting up in front of the temples, and the echo of footsteps on brick in the lanes.

Data notes: the tile keeps the patan-core zone with a default of four storeys. The temples around the square carry height tags of about one metre, so they are given real storey counts, temple brick and pitched roofs. The palace blocks and the Kumari house keep brick with flat roofs. The hospital, the mall, the schools, the bank and the shop buildings in the south are set to flat-roofed institutional or RCC. Nine footprints under 5 m² around the square are removed. Shrine props are placed at the worship points that are not drawn as buildings (the others sit inside a footprint that is already styled as a temple); the Mahabuddha temple is left to its own landmark card.
