# x29_y12 — इतछें (Itachhen)

- The tile covers the heart of old Bhaktapur: Bhaktapur Durbar Square in the north-west, Taumadhi Square in the centre, and the brick lanes that run south toward Talako Tole (Pottery Square). Almost every building is Newar brick, three to four storeys, tightly packed along lanes only a few metres wide.
- Bhaktapur Durbar Square faces south. On its north side stand the Palace of 55 Windows and the Golden Gate; in front of them are the stone Vatsala and Siddhi Laxmi temples, the octagonal Chyasilin Mandap and the Lion Gate statue. Much of it was rebuilt after the 2015 earthquake.
- Taumadhi Square sits just south of Durbar Square. The five-roofed Nyatapola dominates it at about 30 m, the tallest pagoda in Nepal, with the boxier Bhairabnath temple beside it.
- Talako Tole (Pottery Square), south-west of Taumadhi, is an open court where Prajapati potters throw clay on wheels and dry bowls and lamps in the sun.
- Sensory detail: the smell of wet clay and woodsmoke, bells from the small shrines at every corner, and the scrape of pottery being carried across brick paving.

Data notes: several OSM temples carry a `height` of 0.85–2 m (a plinth height, not a building height); they are overridden with storey counts, and Nyatapola with a 30 m height. Eleven small footprints drawn across the pedestrian lanes at the east edge are removed. The palace footprints along the south side of Mul Chowk are styled as Newar brick.
