# x40_y07 — बाडे टोल (Bade Tol, Banepa)

- The tile covers the old part of Banepa, a Newar trading town about 25 km east of Kathmandu at roughly 1,500 m. Bade Tol is one of its neighbourhoods. The name Banepa is read as "place of business": the town sat on the trade route to Tibet, and the lane that cuts through the tile is still called Bhont Marg after Bhonta, an old name of the town.
- About 2,500 footprints are mapped, packed along lanes that are 2 to 4 m wide in the old bazaar. Brick houses of two to three storeys sit among newer RCC blocks, and the brick is styled along the old trade lanes (Bhont Marg, Wasinkhyo, Japali, Kwon Lal).
- Two main roads cross the tile: the Araniko Highway (trunk, drawn 14 m wide) in the south-west, and the Bhaktapur–Nala–Banepa road (primary) running from the north edge down through the town to the junction at about (416, 722). Signs, awnings and taxis are placed there.
- Local tradition describes Banepa as a walled town with eight Ganesh gates and eight ponds. Many small Ganesh and Chandeshwori shrines are mapped in the lanes, plus the Kobha pond at the south-east edge.
- The terrain is a gentle hillside: the 566 m² Chandeshwori footprint and Mayur Cinema sit on ground that varies by 4 to 5 m across the footprint, so their bases follow the lowest corner and no wall floats.

Data notes: 13 footprints are removed (7 under 8 m² and 6 lying more than half under a road). Footprints under 20 m² are one storey and under 35 m² two storeys. Worship buildings are styled by id as brick temples; greenhouses, garages and the workshop are one-storey tin sheds. Worship POIs with no footprint get a shrine prop; stupas get prayer flags.
