# x15_y17 — भोटाहिटी (Bhotahity)

- The tile covers the north-eastern edge of old Kathmandu: the Bhotahity and Asan bazaar lanes on the west side, Ratna Park and the Tundikhel parade ground in the middle, Rani Pokhari with the Ghantaghar clock tower, and the Kamaladi streets to the east. Bhotahity is named after a stone water spout; "Bhota" is the Newari word for Lhasa, and the neighbourhood sits on the old trade route to Tibet. Today it is a crowded market for books, stationery, clothes and seeds, between Ratna Park and Asan.
- Rani Pokhari is a rectangular pond dug in 1670 by King Pratap Malla. A causeway from the western bank leads to the Bal Gopaleshwar Mahadev temple in the middle; the four corners of the pond carry smaller shrines, and a stone elephant carrying the king and his sons stands on the south bank. The pond and temple were rebuilt in brick and reopened in 2020.
- Tundikhel was once a parade ground two to three miles long; in this tile it survives as the open grass of Ratna Park and Peace Park, which break the dense roof pattern and leave the wide gaps in the street shot.
- Kanti Path (14 m, four lanes, one-way) and Darbar Marg carry the traffic; Bagbazar Sadak and Kamaladi Marg are lined with offices, banks, training institutes and shops. The 78 m Skywalk Tower, a glass viewing tower, stands in Kamaladi at the east edge.
- Large institutions fill the middle and south: Bir Hospital, the Sainik Hospital, the National Trauma Center, Durbar High School, the Rastriya Naach Ghar theatre and the police commissioner's office.
- Sensory detail: shop-radio music and book-sellers' calls in Bhotahity, with the Ghantaghar clock tower and scooter horns beyond the park.

Data notes: 23 footprints under 8 m² are removed as slivers. A gatehouse across New Road, a house footprint across a lane near Asan and a "bridge" box on the roadway in Kanti Path are removed. The footbridge to the Rani Pokhari temple is a 72 m footprint; it is restyled as a 0.9 m brick slab. Temples are styled as brick with pitched roofs; hospitals, schools, colleges, police and theatres are institutional; banks, malls and buildings of seven storeys or more are glass or white plaster.
