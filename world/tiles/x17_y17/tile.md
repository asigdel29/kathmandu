# x17_y17 — मैतीदेवी (Maitidevi)

- Maitidevi is a dense residential and institutional neighbourhood east of the old core, around 1,300 m above sea level, sharing its ward with Gyaneshwor. Four-storey RCC blocks stand shoulder to shoulder, with colleges, schools, banks and small hotels filling the lanes between the main roads.
- The tile is cut by Pashupati Sadak, which enters at the north-west corner and runs east as a four-lane secondary road, and by Dillibazar Marg, which enters at the south-east and runs west towards Dilli Bazaar. Maiti Devi Marg links the two through the middle of the tile.
- The neighbourhood is named after the Maitidevi temple, a Hindu shrine to the goddess; it is described as built in woodwork and metal craft rather than the usual Kathmandu pagoda style. In the data it is a point on Maiti Devi Marg with no building footprint, so it is marked with a shrine prop.
- Many of the footprints are education buildings (colleges and schools tagged with 3–6 storeys), and the German embassy compound sits on Gyaneshwor Marga near the top of the tile.
- Sensory detail: the horn-and-brake noise of Pashupati Sadak traffic, school-hour crowds on the side lanes, and small Ganesh shrines tucked at corners.

Data notes: one fuel-station footprint was drawn on top of its own forecourt service road and is removed. Building heights default to four storeys here; tagged levels are kept. Colleges, the embassy and the survey office use the institutional style; banks use the glass commercial style.
