# x14_y17 — मखन (Makhan)

- The tile covers the old royal core of Kathmandu: Basantapur (Kathmandu) Durbar Square in the south-west and the lanes of Makhan Tole, Indra Chowk and Kel Tole running north-east from it. It is dense brick-and-timber Newar city, four storeys is typical.
- Makhan Tole is the old diagonal trade lane that leaves the north-east corner of the square for Indra Chowk and Asan; today it is busy with scooters, shop fronts and small shrines at nearly every corner.
- The square is a Kathmandu Valley World Heritage monument zone; its temples and palace courts date mostly from the 16th–18th centuries, and Kasthamandap was rebuilt after the 2015 earthquake.
- Indra Chowk sits below the Akash Bhairab temple, with shops on the ground floor and the shrine above.
- Kel Tole has the Seto Machhindranath (Janma Dyo) bahal: a courtyard with a gilded temple, brass lamps and stone lions.
- Sensory detail: brass bells, scooter horns and the smell of incense and spices in lanes only a few metres wide.

Data notes: several OSM temples carry a `height` of 0.5–1 m (a plinth height, not a building height); they are overridden with storey counts. Three footprints drawn across roads are removed. The Durbar Square landmarks themselves are modelled separately; this tile only keeps their footprints in brick style.
